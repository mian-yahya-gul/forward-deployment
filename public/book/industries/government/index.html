Government

Public sector organizations often serve the largest volume of repetitive, rules-based case processing of any sector — permit applications, benefits eligibility, records requests — under public accountability standards that make transparency non-negotiable. If you lead an agency, department, or program office, the workflows below will probably feel familiar — and each one is a candidate for the kind of engagement described at the end of this chapter.

Where time leaks today​

  • Permit and licensing review. Applications are checked manually against code and regulation, with cycle times that frustrate applicants and consume reviewer capacity.
  • Benefits eligibility determination. Eligibility rules are complex and change frequently, and manual case review struggles to keep pace with application volume.
  • Public records requests. Locating, reviewing, and redacting documents in response to records requests is a largely manual, deadline-driven process.
  • Citizen service inquiries. Call centers and front counters field high volumes of routine questions about status, requirements, and process.
  • Grants and procurement compliance. Verifying compliance documentation across vendors and grant recipients is repetitive and audit-sensitive.
  • Code enforcement and inspections. Scheduling, tracking, and following up on code violations and inspection results is managed across disconnected systems, and repeat violations are hard to spot without someone manually cross-referencing case history.
  • Interagency data sharing. Cases that touch multiple agencies — a benefits case that also involves housing or health services, for instance — require staff to manually re-enter or re-request the same information across systems that don't talk to each other.
  • Public meeting and comment processing. Transcribing, summarizing, and routing public comments and meeting records for the public record is a manual, time-sensitive task that competes with everything else on a clerk's desk.
  • Fraud, waste, and abuse detection. Benefits and grant programs are exposed to fraud and improper payments, but catching a pattern usually depends on an investigator noticing an anomaly by hand or a citizen tip — not a systematic review of the full caseload.
  • Appeals and hearings preparation. When a citizen appeals a benefits, licensing, or code enforcement decision, assembling the case file — prior determinations, correspondence, supporting documentation — for the hearing officer is a manual research task that happens under the same statutory deadlines as everything else on a caseworker's desk.

Where forward deployment fits​

A Digital FTE can pre-screen permit applications against current code, flag missing documentation immediately instead of after a multi-week review cycle, and route only substantively complete applications to a reviewer's queue. In benefits processing, a system can apply eligibility rules consistently across the full caseload and flag edge cases for human adjudication, reducing both backlog and inconsistency between caseworkers. In records requests, a Digital FTE can locate and pre-review responsive documents, leaving the redaction and release decision with a records officer.

In code enforcement, a system can track violation and inspection history across properties and flag repeat or escalating patterns that a single case file wouldn't reveal on its own. In interagency cases, a Digital FTE can — within your existing data-sharing and privacy authorizations — assemble the relevant history from connected systems so a caseworker isn't asking a citizen to re-explain their situation to a fourth different office.

For public meetings and comment processing, a system can produce first-pass transcripts and summaries and route comments to the correct department automatically, leaving a clerk to verify accuracy and handle anything sensitive rather than transcribing from scratch.

Fraud and improper-payment detection follows the same pattern as benefits eligibility: a Digital FTE can screen the full caseload continuously against known fraud patterns and flag anomalies for investigator review, instead of relying on an investigator to notice a pattern in the cases that happen to cross their desk. For appeals and hearings, a system can assemble the case file — prior determinations, correspondence, supporting documentation — automatically, so a hearing officer starts from a complete record instead of a caseworker reconstructing it under deadline.

What stays human​

Every determination that affects a citizen's legal rights, benefits, or access — eligibility decisions, permit approvals, redaction judgment calls — remains with accountable public employees. The system's role is to make the process faster and more consistent for everyone in the queue, with full transparency into how it arrived at any recommendation, never to make the determination itself.

A Digital FTE might flag that an application is missing a required document or that a property has a pattern of repeat violations, but the approval, denial, or enforcement action is always issued by a person who can be held accountable for it — which is also, not incidentally, what public trust in these processes requires.

Signals you're ready​

Agencies tend to see the fastest results from a first engagement when they already have:

  • Permit or benefits backlogs that have become a public or political issue.
  • Records request response times that routinely miss statutory deadlines.
  • Code enforcement cases where repeat violations aren't being caught until a citizen complains again.
  • Caseworkers manually re-keying the same citizen information across multiple agency systems.
  • A public comment or records process that depends on a small team working significant overtime around deadlines.
  • Fraud or improper-payment cases that surface mainly through citizen tips rather than systematic review.
  • Appeals and hearings preparation that regularly runs up against the statutory deadline.

What a first engagement looks like​

A typical first engagement follows the same five phases described in Part One, applied to your own operations:

  • Discover. We spend time with the caseworkers, clerks, and inspectors actually processing permits, benefits, or records requests — not just the department directors who sponsor the project — to map how the work really happens today, where the backlogs are, and which systems are involved.
  • Prioritize. Every candidate workflow gets scored against how much it affects backlog, statutory deadline risk, or public trust, and how ready the underlying data actually is. The result is a short, ranked list — usually one program or workflow — rather than an open-ended AI wish list.
  • Design. For the workflow at the top of that list, we design a Digital FTE with your public-records, privacy, and due-process requirements built in from the start: what it's allowed to flag versus decide, what always routes to an accountable public employee, and how its output gets logged for transparency.
  • Deploy. The Digital FTE goes live inside your existing case management, permitting, or benefits system — not a separate tool staff have to remember to check — starting with a single program or department so it can be validated against real cases before wider rollout.
  • Optimize. Once it's live, we track the metrics that matter to your agency — cycle time, backlog size, deadline compliance — and keep refining the system as edge cases surface, rather than treating go-live as the finish line.
