Airline & Ticketing

Airline commercial operations run a fundamentally different business than aviation operations does — reservations, fares, loyalty, and passenger service — and it runs on exactly the kind of high-volume, rules-heavy, exception-driven workflow that forward deployment is built for. If you lead commercial operations, reservations, or revenue management, the workflows below will probably feel familiar — and each one is a candidate for the kind of engagement described at the end of this chapter.

Where time leaks today​

  • Reservation and booking support. A large share of contact center volume is routine booking changes, seat requests, and fare questions that agents resolve by working the same reservation system a passenger could largely self-serve if the interface for it were better.
  • Irregular operations (IROPS) rebooking. When weather or a mechanical issue disrupts a schedule, the volume of passengers needing rebooking spikes exactly when staff capacity is most constrained, and manual rebooking prioritizes whoever calls first rather than who has the most time-sensitive itinerary.
  • Fare and revenue management support. Building and auditing fare rules across markets, seasons, and partner airlines is a detailed, rules-heavy task that revenue analysts do largely by hand against pricing systems that don't flag inconsistencies on their own.
  • Baggage claims and compensation processing. Delayed, lost, or damaged baggage claims require cross-referencing flight records, baggage tracking data, and passenger documentation — a process that's manual enough that resolution time is a recurring source of complaints.
  • Loyalty program servicing. Mileage disputes, tier status questions, and redemption issues consume significant service capacity, particularly when a booking or partner-airline data discrepancy requires manual reconciliation.
  • Group and corporate booking management. Group reservations, corporate travel accounts, and their associated payment and documentation requirements are tracked with heavier manual involvement than individual bookings, and errors are costly given the size of each transaction.
  • Interline and codeshare reconciliation. Passenger and revenue data for itineraries spanning partner airlines requires reconciliation across systems that don't share a single source of truth, and discrepancies are typically caught during settlement rather than at booking.
  • Regulatory and denied-boarding compliance. Compensation obligations for delays, cancellations, and denied boarding vary by jurisdiction and route, and calculating the correct obligation for each affected passenger is a detailed, rules-based task usually done under time pressure during a disruption.
  • Overbooking and volunteer management. When a flight is oversold, soliciting volunteers, calculating escalating compensation offers, and documenting who accepted what happens live at the gate under time pressure, and is handled largely ad hoc by whichever agent is working the flight.
  • Payment fraud and chargeback review. Card-not-present fraud screening on ticket purchases and chargeback response for disputed bookings both require assembling booking history, passenger verification, and travel-usage data against a tight response window — a process that scales with ticket volume, not analyst headcount.

Where forward deployment fits​

A Digital FTE can resolve the large share of routine booking, seat, and fare-question volume directly against your reservation system, escalating only the cases that need a human judgment call — a fare exception, a sensitive travel situation, a genuinely ambiguous policy question. During irregular operations specifically, a system can rebook the affected passenger population automatically according to your prioritization policy (connection risk, fare class, status tier) far faster than a queue worked in call order, and proactively notify passengers of their new itinerary instead of waiting for them to call in.

In revenue management, a Digital FTE can continuously audit fare rules across markets and partner agreements for inconsistencies, flagging what a person should look at rather than requiring analysts to sample-check a ruleset too large to review manually. In baggage claims, a system can cross-reference tracking data, flight records, and passenger documentation automatically and resolve straightforward claims without a person touching the file, reserving human attention for claims that are disputed or high-value.

For loyalty servicing, a Digital FTE can reconcile mileage and status discrepancies against booking and partner-airline data automatically, closing the routine cases and surfacing only genuine disputes. In interline and codeshare settlement, a system can flag data mismatches between partner airlines at the point they occur rather than at settlement, when they're far more expensive and slower to unwind. And for regulatory compensation, a Digital FTE can calculate the correct obligation per passenger per jurisdiction automatically during a disruption, ensuring consistent, auditable compliance instead of ad hoc case-by-case judgment made under time pressure.

Overbooking management follows a similar logic to IROPS rebooking: a Digital FTE can run the volunteer solicitation and compensation-offer process against your policy in real time, tracking who's been offered what and at which price point, so gate agents manage the conversation instead of the math. For payment fraud and chargebacks, a system can screen bookings against known fraud patterns at the point of sale and assemble the evidence packet for a disputed chargeback automatically, flagging only the cases that need a person's judgment call.

What gets connected​

  • Passenger Service Systems (PSS) — reservations, inventory, and departure-control data in one core platform.
  • Reservation systems — booking records and fare rules.
  • Departure Control Systems (DCS) — check-in, boarding, and weight-and-balance data.
  • Revenue management systems — pricing and inventory-control decisions.
  • Global Distribution Systems (GDS) — travel-agency and third-party booking channels.
  • Customer relationship management (CRM) — passenger profiles and service history.
  • Loyalty platforms — frequent-flyer accounts and redemption activity.
  • Baggage tracking systems — bag status and mishandling records.
  • Payment and fraud systems — transaction processing and fraud-flagging data.
  • Airport operational systems — gate, ramp, and turnaround status.
  • Partner / interline settlement systems — codeshare and interline billing data.
  • Contact-center platforms — passenger inquiries and case history.

For an airline, that means a Digital FTE works inside your PSS, DCS, and revenue management systems directly — not a separate tool an agent has to check between the ones they already use. DeosAI connects intelligence into the systems that already run your operation, rather than asking your team to adopt a new platform.

What stays human​

Any decision involving a safety-related irregular operation, a compensation dispute a passenger contests, or a sensitive passenger situation (medical, bereavement, security) remains with trained service staff. The system's role is to handle the high-volume, rules-based majority of transactions so that human agents are available — and not already buried in routine volume — when a passenger genuinely needs one.

A Digital FTE might rebook the majority of an affected flight's passengers automatically during a disruption, but a passenger who disputes their new itinerary, has a documented medical need, or is affected by a denied-boarding situation talks to a person. Revenue management recommendations are reviewed by an analyst before they change live pricing; nothing changes fares or policy without a human decision behind it.

Signals you're ready​

This tends to be a strong first engagement where an airline or travel provider already sees:

  • Contact center volume dominated by routine, rules-based transactions rather than genuinely novel requests.
  • IROPS events where rebooking speed and prioritization consistency are a recurring source of complaints.
  • A revenue management team that audits fare rules by sampling rather than exhaustively, for lack of time.
  • Baggage claim resolution times that are a recurring driver of customer dissatisfaction.
  • Interline settlement discrepancies that are caught late and are expensive or slow to reconcile.
  • Overbooking situations where compensation offers vary noticeably by which agent is working the gate.
  • A chargeback response rate that's slipping past the card network's deadline as ticket volume grows.

What a first engagement looks like​

A typical first engagement follows the same five phases described in Part One, applied to your own operations:

  • Discover. We spend time with the reservations, contact center, and revenue management staff actually handling bookings, IROPS, or claims — not just the commercial leaders who sponsor the project — to map how the work really happens today, where the backlogs are, and which systems are involved.
  • Prioritize. Every candidate workflow gets scored against how much it affects passenger experience, revenue leakage, or compliance risk, and how ready the underlying data actually is. The result is a short, ranked list — usually one or two workflows — rather than an open-ended AI wish list.
  • Design. For the workflow at the top of that list, we design a Digital FTE with your fare rules, compensation policy, and regulatory requirements built in from the start: what it's allowed to resolve on its own, what always routes to a person, and how its actions get logged.
  • Deploy. The Digital FTE goes live inside your existing reservation system, PSS, or revenue management platform — not a separate tool staff have to remember to check — starting with a single market, flight category, or ticket type so it can be validated against real cases before wider rollout.
  • Optimize. Once it's live, we track the metrics that matter to your team — resolution time, rebooking speed during disruptions, chargeback win rate — and keep refining the system as edge cases surface, rather than treating go-live as the finish line.

Where to start with DeosAI Labs​

If any of the above sounds familiar, here's where a conversation with us usually starts, depending on which workflow is hurting most:

  • Intelligent Business Workflows. Improve operational efficiency through workflow automation and decision support. Best if: IROPS rebooking, baggage claims, interline reconciliation, or regulatory compensation calculations are where the backlog lives.
